## Important corrections to proposal/presentation claims

1. **MAST percentages are version-dependent and neither document's number is the current v3 figure.** [MAST v3 PDF, Figure 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657v3) lists system design 44.2%, inter-agent misalignment 32.3%, verification 23.5% across the analyzed 1,642 traces; combined first two categories total 76.5%. These are study-specific distributions of annotated failure modes, not proof of what causes failures in all multi-agent systems. The first arXiv version analyzed 151 traces and gave system design 37.17%, misalignment 31.41%, verification 31.41%. Therefore do not combine a 1,600+ trace description with an unverified 37%/79% figure. v3 PDF identifies NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks; do not infer spotlight status without official confirmation.

2. **Historical protocols did not guarantee meaning preservation merely by existing.** [Paurobally, Cunningham and Jennings, AAMAS 2005](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~rjc/AAMAS2005.pdf), A Formal Framework for Agent Interaction Semantics, describes FIPA ACL semantics as complicated/contentious and published interaction protocols as ambiguous, incomplete and unverified. The proposal's well-defined-by-construction/old guarantee framing is too strong. Better: symbolic protocols supplied explicit formal semantics under assumptions, while compliance, interpretation and ontology alignment were longstanding unresolved problems.

3. **Framework capability differs from paper experimental configuration.** [AutoGen paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155) supports configurable combinations of LLMs, humans and tools. [Official current model-client documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/core-user-guide/components/model-clients.html) includes OpenAI-compatible/Gemini, Azure, Ollama and Anthropic clients. Therefore it is false to describe all existing frameworks as intrinsically homogeneous/single-vendor. It may be true that particular published experiments use homogeneous teams; cite their actual configurations rather than assuming.

4. **Preservation metrics are established prior art.** [Facts Without Rules full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29028v1) has boundary and operational preservation measures, multiple handoff formats, compression interventions and no-handoff comparisons. Narrow proposed novelty to causal separation of protocol/adapter translation, model/vendor heterogeneity, and lossy summarization while measuring typed capabilities and tool fidelity on objectively graded tasks. That narrower contribution remains a hypothesis requiring a wider literature audit.

5. **A lack of evidence for a benchmark is not proof of absence.** Neither the audited abstracts nor these corrections establish a first-ever claim. A documented systematic search and detailed comparisons to closest papers/code are needed before that claim is defensible.

### Additional claim corrections from this identity check

- BFCL's ICML 2025 abstract explicitly includes stateful multi-step agentic evaluation. Calling BFCL single-agent may be an appropriate scope distinction, but calling it single-turn or implying that tool fidelity is only measured in one-hop tasks is inaccurate. Protocol-crossing agent delegation is the narrower distinction.
- MultiAgentBench explicitly varies coordination protocols, but these are topologies (star, chain, tree, graph), not MCP/A2A adapters. Use transport/protocol adapter comparison when stating our distinction.
- Lost in the Middle measures position effects in long-context use. Cumulative degradation across handoffs is an inference to test, not a finding directly established by that paper.
- Timestamped or fresh tasks limit contamination risk but do not establish contamination freedom for opaque provider training sets. Keep the paper's own terminology separate from the guarantee our benchmark can justify.
- Neither PDF contains a complete formal reference list with editions/venues/pages for every named work. The presentation's 18 papers reviewed statement is not verified by an independently available reading log.
