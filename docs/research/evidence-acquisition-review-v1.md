## Restricted fragment and strongest attaining comparator

Consider a supplied finite set of compatible world/model histories, finitely many actual query bundles and contracts, a bounded query/control horizon, and an explicit public transition/observation relation. Every arm receives the same task bindings, namespace/profile information, complete bundle outcomes, permitted actions, invalidation signals and costs. Each contract retains its declared completion/safety quantifier. A shared terminal predicate requires sound certificates and the same coverage as the comparator; epistemic uncertainty cannot be renamed as a successful decision. A negative certificate still requires complete fresh evidence. Unsupported operations, unavailable replies and unknown future evolution retain uncertainty unless an explicit recovery/dynamics model covers them.

An exact finite contingent-policy baseline uses state `(compatible histories, accepted receipts, binding/profile context, remaining horizon)`. A query is chosen at an OR node; each permitted response and intervening update is an AND branch. At a valid terminal state the remaining value is zero; at an unresolved exhausted horizon it is infinite. Otherwise it minimizes, over allowed queries, the maximum of that outcome's fully charged cost plus its successor value. Induction on the remaining horizon establishes optimal worst-case cost within this supplied finite fragment. With a supplied justified prior, a separate expectation recurrence gives the Bayesian comparator. This is a standard dynamic program, not a candidate contribution or a claim of practical tractability. Include factored/symbolic/backward and cached implementations before asserting a representation or checking-cost advantage. [Bonet and Geffner](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8365/8224), pp.1757–1760, already discuss contingent policies, relevance contexts and factored belief tracking.

Multiple contracts do not force independent testing: jointly classify the required decision vector, or use overlapping regions when alternative certificates are accepted. One returned bundle can serve every applicable contract. An invalidated premise can disable one justification while another valid justification preserves the decision. These facilities must be available to the strongest baseline.

## Source-grounded discrimination boundary

The existing pinned bundle index establishes one task read per task, reusable card/profile observations, and mgcrea's bundled local inventory. Amir offers previously returned opaque handles rather than a generic inventory query. Read-only observations do not expose a common cross-peer epoch, lease, principal-specific invalidation feed or atomic multi-task snapshot. Local cache TTLs and mirror timestamps cannot supply those guarantees. Raw mgcrea access must retain the [documented versioning qualification](reference-bridge-results-v1.md); the authored port's default cannot be assumed for another service.

An admissible future screen would first require a genuine application contract over several already public task bindings and actual shared dependencies on both eligible routes. Compare joint bundle-aware exact policy, EC2/HEC/DIRECT/BISECT where their assumptions apply, dependency/alternative-justification caching, and applicable PC2/AgentThread adaptations. Exercise unchanged evidence, a changed shared premise, one surviving alternative certificate, relevant/irrelevant task changes, unavailable replies and lack of a cross-peer snapshot. Charge profile construction, every returned bundle, updates, proof generation/checking and failed actions equally; keep all decisions/coverage identical. Splitting GetTask into status/context/artifact probes or invalidating all independent certificates in the baseline would fail the screen. No such source-backed application witness, concrete extra technical step or prespecified advantage has been established here, so this screen is **unexecuted and not an experiment authorization**.

The remaining possibility is a concrete representation, approximation or acquisition procedure with proved assumptions and a measurable advantage over those strong comparators. Naming more peers, more contracts, another quantifier, CEGAR, cache invalidation or certificate dependencies does not identify that procedure. Retain this adverse result under the [novelty gate](implementation-novelty-gate.md); it supports bounded screening, not scaling.
